# PDR-0003 — Build, change, approve, and review a complete week

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-08-24
- Owners: Household product

## Decision and reason

Recommend one complete personalized week and repair related changes together.
The product reduces planning work without requiring meal tracking.

A period defaults to seven days with a configurable start day. Adults may replan
only remaining dates. Every managed person, date and occasion needs an explicit
resolution: a meal, prepared output, external meal, intentional skip, flexible
choice or unresolved gap. Unmanaged occasions create no hidden requirements.
Flexible choices are deliberate coverage with no shopping assumption. Gaps must
be resolved before approval and cannot be hidden behind invented meals.

Draft changes repair all affected alternatives, portions, cook events, prepared
components, leftover allocations and shopping preview. Low-risk repairs may be
automatic, but consequential effects, hard constraints, gaps and changed
assumptions stay understandable.

Any adult may edit, approve, reject, reopen or revise the shared plan. Record who
acted and expose the latest change. Dependants cannot approve or edit in the MVP.
Approval pins profile, routine, recipe, portion and plan versions. Later changes
are proposed revisions with understandable differences and effects; an adult
accepts the complete revision before it becomes active.

## During the week and learning

Assume the approved plan happened unless an adult reports an exception. No meal
needs to be marked cooked or eaten. Planned same-week leftovers need no separate
confirmation. A missed producing meal can be reported immediately or at review;
explain the effects on later meals.

Optional review at the next planning session collects useful feedback and never
blocks the next plan. Ask follow-ups only when they improve planning. Signals may
include liking, dislike, skipped or unmade meals, effort, quantity, fallback
outcomes, dependant rejection and make-again requests.

Any adult may report for any person. Preserve subject, reporter, context and the
actual target, which may instead be a meal, cook event, recipe, routine, plan or
the household. Ordinary feedback is household-visible. Self-reported preference
normally outweighs another adult's observation, but conflicting evidence remains
attributed rather than becoming one synthetic household opinion.

One explicit strong statement may justify an immediate proposal; weaker signals
need repetition across periods. Consider recency and context rather than a fixed
thumbs-down threshold. Skipping is not automatically dislike, and excess effort
may concern capacity rather than food preference. Proposals can change profiles,
routines, fallbacks, cadence, portions, recipes or capacity. Enduring changes
require adult confirmation and remain inspectable and reversible.

Cross-week prepared stock must be confirmed before the next plan relies on it.
Ignoring the question leaves it unavailable. Metrics distinguish proposal gaps,
requested changes, automatic repairs and approved revisions.

Mandatory tracking, silent approved-plan rewrites, consensus approval, dependant
self-report accounts, private ordinary feedback, external calendars, full
custody/visitor scheduling and unconfirmed enduring learning remain deferred.
